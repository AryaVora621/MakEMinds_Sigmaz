## Down-cell exit path (derived from scene.json corners, red hive)
- Down cell (far cell in audience_up): opening bottom edge at (y 89.88, z 32.01), floor runs to back-bottom (y 79.47, z 38.02). The floor rises 30 deg going INTO the cell, so it falls 30 deg toward the opening.
- The floor's lowest point is the opening bottom edge itself (same z 32.009). There is no sill or lip in the corner data.
- Mirror check (far_up, audience cell is down): floor from (y 54.12, z 32.01) to (y 64.54, z 38.02). Same result.
- Up cell floor (audience_up audience cell): descends 30 deg going inward (z 53.47 to 47.45). Balls roll to the back, which is the intended hold.

Conclusion for gravity at the two stable tilts: a ball on the floor of the DOWN cell rolls out through the opening under gravity (unless the mesh or a lid stops it). A ball in the UP cell rolls inward and stays. This matches the geometry, so the "balls stay in the down cell" assumption (balls.js / hivecycle.js, "manual 10.5") is NOT supported by the physical geometry as exported. It needs either a physical retaining feature not in the corner data, or the rule is a game rule that the sim enforces by fiat.

- Mid-tilt (hive at 0 deg between the stable states): floor incline varies linearly with hive angle (rigid rotation about X), so the down-cell floor is horizontal at the midpoint and any ball there sits on a flat floor. This is derived by linearity, not measured.
- Sphere-on-incline fact (standard rigid-body mechanics, not sourced in this repo): a sphere cannot rest on a 30 deg slope; it rolls. Source: unverified (no URL).
