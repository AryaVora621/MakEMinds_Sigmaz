# t07: BallWorld integrator and tunnelling audit (read-only analysis)

Scope: `web/js/balls.js` (BallWorld.step, _substep, contact, _hiveSweep, _hiveCollide, _boxCollide, _robotCollide, _ballBall), `web/js/hivemesh.js` (TriMesh.sweepSegment), `web/js/app.js` (SHOOTER, updateBalls), `web/js/shotrules.js` (speedFromRpm), `web/data/robot_spec.json`, `web/data/colliders.json`, `web/data/hive_collider.bin`.
Method: code reading, plus a read-only Node script (scratchpad only, no project writes) for mesh and collider extents. NOT run: no sim, no tests, no screenshots. All arithmetic below is read-only analysis.

## 1. Maximum ball speed
- Launch speed (shotrules.js:133): v = rpm / rpmScale * 2*pi*wheelR*eff / 60, massScale 1 for pollen, sqrt(0.025/0.041)=0.781 for nectar (balls.js:38-45, ASSUMED mass).
- app.js SHOOTER: wheelR 1.89 in, eff 0.45, rpmScale 1.0, maxRpm 6000. robot_spec.json shooter.flywheelRadius 1.89 agrees.
- Wheel-surface speed per rpm = 2*pi*1.89*0.45/60 = 0.08906 in/s per rpm.
- Pollen at maxRpm 6000: **534.4 in/s**. Nectar at 6000: 417.3 in/s.
- Sandbox free aim (aimcontrol.js:200) allows rpm up to 1.5 x maxRpm = 9000: **801.6 in/s** pollen.
- Robot velocity is added at launch (shotrules.js:149-151). DRIVE.maxSpeed = 60 (app.js:38, units assumed in/s, UNVERIFIED). Adds up to ~60 in/s, so ~594 in/s for a driven shot.
- Hard clamp BALL.maxSpeed = 1800 in/s (balls.js:71, applied only inside _substep integration, not after contacts).
- Worst design speed used below: 1800 in/s (clamp), with 802 in/s as the realistic max.

## 2. Substep count and travel per substep
- app.js:452 calls `world.step(dt)` with dt = min(frame dt, 0.05) (app.js:1077). stepFixed exists but app.js does not call it (grep: no `stepFixed` in app.js).
- balls.js:447: n = min(120, max(1, ceil(dt/0.004), ceil(dt*vmax/0.8))), h = dt/n. vmax = max over awake free balls of |v| + G*dt, and also hive tip speed and robot motion.
- Travel per substep is capped at 0.8 in by construction (maxStepTravel). The n<=120 cap binds only when vmax > 5760 in/s, which the 1800 clamp prevents.
- At dt = 1/60 (G*dt = 6.44 in/s):
  - v = 534.4: vmax 540.8, n = ceil(11.27) = 12, h = 1/720 s, travel = **0.75 in** per substep.
  - v = 801.6: vmax 808.0, n = 17, travel = **0.79 in**.
  - v = 1800: vmax 1806.4, n = 38, travel = **0.79 in**.

## 3. Thinnest colliders vs travel (0.75 to 0.79 in)
- Ball radius: pollen 1.391 in (diameter 2.782); nectar 1.7995 in.
- Field walls (bounds, colliders.json: 1.326 in inset, wallHeight undefined so full height): half-space contacts (balls.js:521-527). To cross the plane a centre must move from > r to < 0 away from it, i.e. > 1.391 in in one substep. Travel 0.79 < 1.391. No tunnelling.
- Boxes (_boxCollide, sphere vs AABB): thinnest raw colliders.json box extent is 1.98 in (hive_frame, x). Collision zone is the box grown by r, so the effective slab is 1.98 + 2*1.391 = 4.76 in. Travel 0.79 is 17% of that. No tunnelling. Caveat: measured on the raw file, before app.js:111 filtering and trimFlower.
- Circles: colliders.json has 0 circles.
- Hive mesh (hive_collider.bin, 13,624 tris per tip, 27,248 total): a zero-thickness surface, so "wall thickness" does not apply. Two protections:
  - Sphere contacts (_hiveCollide, sphereContacts, 4 iterations) catch the centre within r of a triangle. Crossing the surface needs the centre to go from distance > r to < -r, i.e. 2.78 in in one substep. Travel is 0.79. No tunnelling.
  - Swept centre segment (_hiveSweep -> sweepSegment, Moller-Trumbore over a grid, cell 2.5 in) also catches centre crossings.
  - Thinnest triangle: min altitude 0.00027 in, min edge 0.00037 in (slivers, 76 tris with altitude < 0.001 in). These create numerical risk for the sweep (det epsilon 1e-12) but not a tunnel path, since the sphere contact test does not depend on triangle quality.
  - Intentional exception: _entering() exempts balls entering the opening from rim contact. This is a designed opening, not tunnelling.
- Ball-ball (_ballBall, _pair): closing speed at 1800 in/s each gives 3600 in/s relative, per-substep 1.58 in. Passing requires 2r = 2.78 in. No tunnelling.

## 4. Verdict
- With the stated speeds (534 to 802 in/s, clamp 1800) and dt = 1/60, tunnelling through walls, boxes, the hive mesh or other balls is NOT possible under the current design. The per-substep travel is 0.75 to 0.79 in, versus 1.39 in radius and 2.78 in diameter.
- Margins shrink in three cases the substep count does not cover:
  1. vmax excludes speed added in contacts. Read-only estimate: a 534 in/s ball struck by a robot closing at 60 in/s with e = 0.35 can leave at about (1+e)*594 - 60 = 742 in/s. Travel then becomes ~1.03 in per substep (742/720). Still below 2r for surfaces (2.78) and balls, and below the 4.76 in box slab. Margin ~2.7x. Not verified by running.
  2. Robot motion is not in vmax (only interpolated when > 40 in per frame, balls.js:445).
  3. The 1800 clamp is applied only at integration, not after contacts.
- Sleep and capture rules (sleepSpeed 1.5, captureSlow 30) are unaffected by this analysis.
