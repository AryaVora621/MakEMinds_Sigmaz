- One team (14343), one robot (Ri3D per the earlier WebSearch summary, not verified in-thread), one fixed solid hood, 43 shots.
- The post itself says the sample is too thin: "we don't really have enough data to tell" (quoted in search snippet, unverified in-thread).
- The 44% is an accumulated count, not a per-shot probability model. It is not an FTC or season-wide figure.
- Post 12 also says the team was testing whether backspin must be mitigated. No backspin number is given in the post.

## 3. What the sim models that controls bounce-out (code read)

Files read: web/js/balls.js (full), web/js/hivecycle.js (full).

balls.js, BALL constants (lines 49-72):
- restitutionHive: 0.3 (ASSUMED). Applied to ball-vs-hive mesh contacts (_hiveCollide line 754, _hiveSweep line 733). Main lever for a shot that hits the hive rim or back wall.
- restitutionWall: 0.55 (SOURCED weak, hard plastic on concrete). Used for field walls and boxes, not the hive mesh.
- slideFriction 0.5 (ASSUMED), restSpeed 5 in/s. Friction on impacts reduces tangential speed, which affects whether a ball slides into the cell.
- dragK 0.0004 (ASSUMED, tuned to a project test). Changes incoming speed at the hive.
- maxSpeed 1800 in/s clamp (not a bounce control at shot speeds).
- Pollen radiusIn 1.391 (nectar 1.7995). Radius sets the rim-grow test (distToPolyEdge < r) and wall clearance.

balls.js, capture logic:
- Entry test _entering (lines 700-710): a ball is "entering" if its centre moved into the cell, is within ENTRY_DEPTH = 3 in behind the opening, and its centre is inside the opening polygon grown by its radius. Entering balls skip hive-mesh collision (lines 726, 748). Bounce-out depends on this grown-polygon test. Changing the radius term or ENTRY_DEPTH changes how many rim hits are forgiven.
- _hiveCapture (757-772): a shot ball is captured only after crossing the opening (_enter flag). The slow-rest rule (captureRest 0.15 s, captureSlow 30 in/s) applies only to start-field balls (b.start). So a shot that rests in the cell without crossing the opening is NOT captured. This is the sim's direct bounce-out rule.
- Opening geometry comes from env.hives.<a>.cells (opening_polygon, opening_corners) and the mesh (hive_collider.bin). These are the controlling geometry inputs.
- Hive tip angle: setHiveAngles / cadAngle (-30 audience_up). Tilt changes the opening angle to the incoming ball (hive pose sensitivity).

balls.js, not modelled:
- No Magnus / spin term. Only drag is applied (line 500-503). So backspin, which the 14343 team said needed mitigation, cannot change the sim's trajectory or bounce-out. Any sim bounce-out number will not reflect backspin.
- No hood or shooter model in balls.js. Launch velocity is set by the caller (shot_core / shotrules).

hivecycle.js:
- Does NOT control bounce-out. It only counts balls that reach a cell (addScoredBall, called from onCapture after capture). Its tip rule (HIVE_PARAMS: ballsToTip 8, mode 'weight', nectarWeight 1.75, startingNectar 3 uncounted) decides tips, not whether a ball bounces. Note: HIVE_PARAMS startingNectar comment says the 3 starting nectar do not count toward tips.
- A bounce-out in the sim shows up only as the absence of an onCapture call. hivecycle never sees it.

## 4. Parameters that would set the bounce-out rate in the sim (summary)

1. restitutionHive (0.3, ASSUMED): primary lever. Higher COR means more rim bounce.
2. Opening geometry: opening_polygon, opening_corners, and the grown-polygon radius (ENTRY_DEPTH 3 in, radius r). Largest effect on rim vs. entry.
3. Shot speed and angle (caller-supplied, shot_core/shotrules). Also dragK (0.0004, ASSUMED).
4. slideFriction (0.5, ASSUMED) and restSpeed (5 in/s): affect whether a rim hit slides in or stops.
5. Hive tip angle (cadAngle, setHiveAngles): changes the opening angle.
6. Not present: spin/Magnus, hood geometry, pollen mass (mass is unitless 1.0 in balls.js).

To calibrate against the 44% number, the sim would need the 14343 shooter conditions (speed, angle, hood, backspin), which are not in the thread. Calibrating restitutionHive to 44% would be a fit to one unverified number, and it would not be a measured value.
